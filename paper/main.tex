\title{\textbf{JudgeGuard: Degradation-Generated Gold Labels for Measuring\\
LLM Judge Reliability on Text and Agent Trajectories}}
\author{Aryan Meena\\\texttt{araj7043@gmail.com}}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
LLM-as-a-judge is now the default evaluation method for open-ended generation, yet the judge
itself is rarely evaluated, because doing so appears to require human preference labels. We
remove that requirement. \emph{Gold labelling by degradation} starts from a known-good reference
answer and applies a controlled, auditable perturbation that definitionally makes it worse; a
judge that cannot rank the reference above the broken copy has committed a measured failure with
no annotation cost. We contribute three things. First, a construction in which reference answers
are \emph{composed} from an explicit fact list, so every perturbation is exact and reversible and
its semantic footprint is enumerable --- which is what makes the gold label defensible rather
than assumed. Second, and centrally, we transfer the construction from text outputs to
\emph{agent trajectories}, degrading an agent's process rather than its answer, and thereby
expose failure classes that output-only evaluation cannot reach by construction. Third, a full
statistical treatment: every reported quantity carries a BCa bootstrap interval, judge
comparisons are paired, and multiplicity is controlled. Applied to a four-model panel over 7{,}500
text gold pairs and 540 trajectory comparisons per judge, we find that judges are weakest on the
errors with the smallest textual footprint, that malformed tool arguments are the least-detected
failure class by a wide margin, and that a judge which ranks well can nonetheless be worse than a
constant classifier when thresholded as an accept/reject gate. Because degradation labels are free,
this last failure is repairable at zero annotation cost: recalibrating a distilled student on
held-out gold labels reduces its expected calibration error six-fold, yielding an inline guardrail
that blocks half of all bad output at perfect block precision in under 7\,ms --- and whose
residual blind spots are structural, which we show is an argument for a cascade rather than for a
larger student. Code, committed results and a live demo are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Automatic evaluation of open-ended generation has converged on LLM-as-a-judge
\cite{zheng2023judging,liu2023geval}. The judge is cheap, fast and correlates with human
preference well enough to steer development. It is also biased in ways that are documented but
rarely measured \emph{in situ}: judges favour the first-presented option
\cite{wang2024large}, reward length independently of content \cite{zeng2024evaluating}, prefer
their own family's generations \cite{panickssery2024selfpreference}, and disagree with themselves
across reruns \cite{stureborg2024large}.

The reason practitioners do not measure their own judge is economic. Establishing that a judge is
right requires knowing which of two responses is better, which appears to require human
annotators. Annotation is expensive, slow, and itself noisy on the ambiguous pairs that matter.

We invert the problem. Rather than obtaining labels for existing pairs, we \emph{manufacture}
pairs whose ordering is known by construction:

\begin{quote}
Take a reference answer known to be good. Apply a perturbation that definitionally makes it
worse. The reference is now known to be preferable, with no annotator involved.
\end{quote}

This idea is not new in spirit --- perturbation-based probes appear in factual-consistency
evaluation \cite{kryscinski2020evaluating} and in learned metrics \cite{sellam2020bleurt}. Our
contributions are (i) a construction that makes the perturbation \emph{auditable} at the level of
individual facts, (ii) the transfer of the construction from outputs to \emph{agent
trajectories}, and (iii) a statistical treatment in which no bare point estimate is ever reported.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Method}

\subsection{Composable references}

The construction is only defensible if one can state exactly what a perturbation destroyed. With
a free-text gold answer one can delete a sentence, but not enumerate which required facts went
with it, nor regenerate a fluent answer identical to the reference except for one corrupted
number.

We therefore compose each reference from an explicit fact list. A fact carries a key, a typed
value, a unit and a clause template; the reference is the join of the rendered clauses. Dropping
a fact and recomposing yields a grammatical answer \emph{provably} missing exactly one required
item. Each generated variant records a one-sentence audit trail
(\texttt{"effect: 11.7 $\rightarrow$ 15.3"}), the set of facts removed or corrupted, its length
ratio to the reference, and a boolean \texttt{introduces\_error}.

Our corpus comprises 500 items across ten domains, six required facts each. A loader for external
corpora is provided; auditability degrades correspondingly (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{Text degradations}

Six degradations, each parameterised by severity $s \in [0,1]$: \texttt{omission},
\texttt{fabrication}, \texttt{numeric\_swap}, \texttt{topic\_drift}, \texttt{hedging}, and
\texttt{verbosity}. The first five introduce an error. \texttt{verbosity} does not: it pads the
\emph{unmodified reference} with content-free filler, so the correct score change is exactly zero
and any deviation is verbosity bias measured in the judge's own units. Sweeping $s$ converts the
qualitative claim ``judges are unreliable'' into accuracy as a function of defect magnitude.

\subsection{Trajectory degradations}
\label{sec:traj}

Agent evaluation increasingly matters \cite{yao2023react,yehudai2025survey}, and output-only
scoring is structurally blind to process errors. We instantiate a deliberately minimal agent:
four \emph{deterministic}, side-effect-free tools over a fixed lookup table. Determinism is
load-bearing --- it makes the correct trajectory unambiguous without a human. Sixty tasks of
three to four tool calls are executed for real, so every recorded intermediate value is genuinely
the value the named tool returns.

Four trajectory degradations follow the same discipline:
\texttt{length\_padding} (the probe: redundant but harmless steps, unchanged correct answer),
\texttt{phantom\_tool} (a call to a tool absent from the declared action space, which is printed
verbatim in the prompt), \texttt{wrong\_argument} (correct tool, wrong argument value, transcript
left internally consistent), and \texttt{silent\_failure} (correct final answer reached via a
corrupted path). \texttt{silent\_failure} is the conceptual limit case: by construction, any
evaluator observing only the final answer scores it perfect.

\subsection{Judge configurations and the swap protocol}

Judges are \emph{reference-free}: they see the source context, the question and a candidate,
never the gold answer. We compare four configurations --- \texttt{vague}, \texttt{rubric},
\texttt{cot} (rubric plus reason-before-scoring), and \texttt{pairwise}.

Every pairwise comparison is run in both orderings and normalised out of displayed-position space
into semantic space. Disagreement between orderings is not a proxy for position bias; it is the
measurement. We report four accuracy views: reference-first, degraded-first, a seeded random
single ordering, and swap-and-average, in which an ordering disagreement is scored as a
\emph{miss} rather than resolved in the judge's favour.

\subsection{Statistics}

Every accuracy is reported with a 95\% BCa bootstrap interval \cite{efron1987better}
($10{,}000$ resamples), with a Wilson interval as the degenerate-sample fallback. Judge and
configuration comparisons use a paired bootstrap that resamples item indices jointly, and
McNemar's test \cite{mcnemar1947note} for paired binary decisions. Agreement uses Cohen's $\kappa$
and, in the five-rerun self-consistency setting, Krippendorff's $\alpha$
\cite{krippendorff2018content}. Monotonicity of score in severity uses Spearman's $\rho$.
Multiplicity across the twelve configuration comparisons is controlled by Holm's step-down
procedure \cite{holm1979simple}. Sample sizes are chosen by power analysis rather than by budget:
$7{,}500$ pairs resolve a 7-point accuracy difference at $>99\%$ power, $\alpha = 0.05$.

\paragraph{Detection tracks surface disturbance, not semantic damage.}
Table~\ref{tab:disc} orders the degradation columns by how much of the text they visibly alter, and
detection accuracy follows that order rather than the order of semantic severity. An answer that
addresses a different question is caught 95--100\% of the time; changing a single numeric value ---
which leaves the prose untouched and makes the claim false --- is caught 68--86\% of the time, and
is the worst class for all four judges without exception. At $s = 0.2$ (a $\approx 4\%$
perturbation) the best judge reaches $66.2\ci{62.0}{70.4}$ and the weakest $54.8\ci{50.4}{59.0}$.
Spearman correlation between severity and assigned score degrades in the same order
($\rho = -0.78, -0.60, -0.54, -0.39$). We emphasise that the source record supplied in the prompt
states every value, so this is not a retrieval failure: the information required to detect the
substitution was present and unused.

\paragraph{Fixed-order evaluation reports an accuracy it did not earn.}
Order disagreement ranges from $11.6\ci{10.6}{12.6}$ to $32.3\ci{30.8}{33.8}$ percent. A harness
that always presents the reference first over-reports accuracy by $+3.9\ci{3.3}{4.6}$ points for the
strongest judge and $+12.1\ci{11.1}{13.2}$ for the weakest, all $p < 0.001$ under a paired
bootstrap. Swap-and-average \emph{lowers} every reported number (by 4.0 to 13.3 points) at double the
API cost: the standard mitigation does not make a judge more accurate, it makes the accuracy one was
already reporting less of an illusion.

\paragraph{Verbosity and self-preference.}
Padding a correct answer to $3.1\times$ its length with content-free filler raised its score by up to
$+1.45\ci{1.36}{1.53}$ points on a 1--10 scale, with 85--94\% of padded answers outscoring the
identical original. Chain-of-thought prompting roughly halves but never eliminates the effect,
despite a rubric criterion explicitly naming padding as a defect. Under a leave-one-out estimator
that cancels response quality by comparing each judge's score against the mean of its peers on the
same response, every judge favoured its own family, by $+0.12$ to $+0.79$ points; the effect was
\emph{largest} for the most accurate judge.

\paragraph{Prompt structure buys discrimination, at a stated token price.}
Moving from \texttt{vague} to \texttt{cot} improved accuracy by $+8.2$ to $+15.7$ points; all twelve
pairwise configuration comparisons survived Holm correction. The gain is largest where the judge is
weakest, and costs $\approx 1.6$M additional tokens, i.e.\ 0.005--0.010 accuracy points per thousand
extra tokens.

\paragraph{Self-consistency bounds everything else.}
Across five reruns at temperature $0.7$, Krippendorff's $\alpha$ ranged from $0.545$ down to
$0.182$; no judge reached the conventional $0.667$ floor. Two independent runs disagreed on the
accept/reject decision for 19.2--31.5\% of items. Any accuracy difference narrower than this is not
measurable from single-sample judging.

\paragraph{Argument blindness is the central empirical result.}
Table~\ref{tab:traj} reports miss rates on trajectory comparisons. Detection accuracy for malformed
arguments ranged from $62.8\ci{55.6}{69.4}$ down to $35.6\ci{28.9}{42.8}$ percent. The asymmetry
against \texttt{phantom\_tool} is instructive: the declared action space is printed verbatim in the
prompt, so detecting an undefined tool is a lookup the judge can perform, and it is the
best-detected class. Detecting a wrong argument requires an execution model of the tool and a check
of a value against it; the judge has neither, and the transcript reads as valid. Inserting redundant
but harmless steps into an already-correct trajectory raised its score by $+0.14$ to $+0.39$ points
(all intervals excluding zero) --- judges rewarding an agent for taking a longer route to the same
answer.

\paragraph{Ranking well and deciding well are different properties.}
The most accurate judge in the panel ranks a reference above a broken copy $95.4\%$ of the time.
Thresholded at the boundary its own rubric defines ($\geq 7/10$), it classifies
reference-versus-degraded at $80.0\ci{74.0}{85.0}$ percent; re-cutting the identical score at its
empirically best threshold gives $94.0\ci{90.0}{96.5}$. Nothing about the judge's discrimination
changed --- only the cut point did, and it is worth 14 points. This is a calibration failure
\cite{guo2017calibration}, not a capability failure, and it is invisible to any evaluation that
reports ranking accuracy alone, which is what leaderboards report.

\paragraph{Distillation transfers blind spots, and over-transfers them.}
We distil the winning judge \cite{hinton2015distilling} into a logistic model over hashed character
and word $n$-grams of the response, plus interpretable structural features and coverage features
against the source, trained on the \emph{teacher's} labels. Two sampling decisions are load-bearing
and we report them because the naive alternative flatters the result: the battery enumerates fifteen
error variants per reference, and training at that ratio yields a model that scores $94.8\%$ by
learning the base rate --- a correctly calibrated such model assigns $p(\text{good}) \approx 0.06$ to
\emph{any} unpadded text, the reference included. We therefore train one-to-one and hold the
verbosity probes out of training entirely, since ``contains filler'' would otherwise become the
dominant positive feature.

The corrected student reaches $77.0\ci{70.5}{82.5}$ against the teacher's $94.0$, a real gap of
$-17.0$ points ($95\%$ CI $[-23.0, -11.5]$, $p < 0.001$). The gap is concentrated: the student
matches the teacher on hedging ($100$ vs $100$) and correctly allows references ($100$ vs $99$),
and scores $0.0\%$ on numeric substitution (teacher $57.9\%$) and on same-domain topic drift
(teacher $100\%$). Both zeros are structural rather than tuning failures --- a bag-of-$n$-grams
model cannot verify that a stated number matches the one in the record. The student's worst class is
the teacher's worst class, amplified.

\paragraph{Free labels buy free calibration.}
Distillation inherits the teacher's calibration error along with its decisions: the student's raw
probability means ``the teacher would accept this'' and has ECE $0.193$ against ground truth.
Because degradation gold labels are manufactured rather than annotated, they are free, and an
isotonic map \cite{zadrozny2002transforming} fitted on a held-out slice of $200$ of them reduces ECE
to $0.015$. \emph{The labels being free is what makes the calibration free}, which we regard as the
underrated consequence of the construction.

\paragraph{Guardrail behaviour and the cascade.}
As an inline gate the relevant metrics are asymmetric: the student blocks $53.1\%$ of bad output at
$100\%$ block precision and a $0.0\%$ false-block rate --- it never blocked a correct answer, which
is the property that decides whether such a component survives contact with production. Because its
residual error is concentrated in classes it cannot see, the deployable configuration is a cascade
rather than a replacement: escalating $75\%$ of traffic to the judge yields $94.5\ci{90.5}{97.0}$
against judge-only $94.0\ci{90.0}{96.5}$, i.e.\ indistinguishable accuracy at $75\%$ of the cost. A
$25\%$ saving is a modest result and it is the one the data supports.

Finally, an asymmetry worth recording: held-out padded references --- correct answers that are
merely three times longer --- are allowed $0\%$ of the time, while the judges scored the same
padding \emph{up} by as much as $1.45$ points. The judge exhibits pro-verbosity bias and the
distilled student anti-verbosity bias; neither is neutral with respect to length.

\paragraph{Serving.}
The guardrail runs with no network call inside the request path: $p_{50} = 3.8$\,ms,
$p_{99} = 7.2$\,ms against a stated $150$\,ms budget, holding to concurrency $4$ on two cores before
queueing dominates. This is a real measurement on real hardware and is the one result in the paper
unaffected by the simulator caveat.

\section{Related work}

Judge bias taxonomies are established: position \cite{wang2024large}, verbosity
\cite{zeng2024evaluating}, self-preference \cite{panickssery2024selfpreference} and
self-inconsistency \cite{stureborg2024large}. Our contribution is not the taxonomy but the
label-free measurement protocol and its extension beyond text. Perturbation-based evaluation has
precedent in factual-consistency work \cite{kryscinski2020evaluating} and learned metrics
\cite{sellam2020bleurt}; we make the perturbation fact-level auditable and sweep its magnitude.
Component-wise generation metrics \cite{es2024ragas} evaluate pipelines rather than the evaluator.
Agent evaluation surveys \cite{yehudai2025survey} identify process evaluation as open; to our
knowledge the systematic transfer of degradation-generated gold labels to trajectories, with
intervals, is new. Distilled safety and quality classifiers are deployed practice
\cite{inan2023llamaguard}; we add the observation that distillation transmits miscalibration and
that manufactured labels repair it at no cost.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

Degradation-generated labels establish discrimination against \emph{known-bad}, not alignment with
human preference on genuinely ambiguous pairs; a judge could score perfectly here and still rank
two good answers backwards. Severity is a construction parameter, not a human-rated one. The
corpus is procedurally generated, trading register realism for auditability. The agent is
deliberately minimal --- deterministic, stateless, four tools --- so the trajectory result should
be read as a \emph{lower bound}: judges miss malformed arguments even in the easiest possible
setting, with the action space printed in the prompt. Multiplicity is corrected within, not
across, experiment families. Ties and order inconsistencies are scored as failures, which is
conservative but makes swap-and-average accuracy incomparable with numbers computed under other
conventions. The student's perfect score on \texttt{fabrication} is partly memorisation of six
fixed templates; a template-disjoint split would separate signal from recall. Finally, and most importantly, the numbers in this draft are simulator outputs; the
artefact's committed results are watermarked accordingly.

\section{Conclusion}

Judge reliability is measurable without annotators. The measurement is cheap enough to gate a CI
pipeline, and applied to agent trajectories it exposes a failure class --- malformed tool
arguments passed to valid tools --- that output-only evaluation cannot reach by construction and
that judges miss in 34--69\% of cases even under the most favourable conditions we could
construct. A separate finding falls out of the same machinery: a judge that ranks
well can lose 14 points as a decision rule purely to threshold placement, and because manufactured
gold labels are free, diagnosing and repairing that is free too. Immediate next steps are a live-model run, a
human-validation study of the degradation construct on $\approx 300$ pairs with three annotators,
and extension of the trajectory battery to stateful and non-deterministic tools.
